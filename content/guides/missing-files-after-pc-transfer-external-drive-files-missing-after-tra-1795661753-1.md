You copied your user folders to an external drive during a PC-to-PC migration, and now the drive shows fewer files than the source.

This is the classic incomplete-copy step: the transfer stopped or skipped items before you could diagnose it. Stay calm and work through it in order.

## Confirm what actually copied

Open the external drive and the source folder side by side. Check the item count and total size of each top-level folder (Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures). If the drive shows fewer items or a smaller size, the copy genuinely did not finish. If counts match but files look absent, you may be viewing a hidden-files issue instead.

## Check for a partial or interrupted copy

Look for folders with the same name but a suffix, or files ending in .tmp or .partial. These indicate the copy was cut off. Also confirm the drive has free space at least equal to the source size; a full drive silently stops mid-transfer.

## Run a verification test

Pick three known files from the source, note their exact sizes, and search for them on the drive. If any are missing or truncated, treat the whole copy as unreliable. This is the controlled diagnosis you were missing, and it explains why you are seeing **external drive files missing after transfer**.

## Roll back and recopy

Do not delete the source. Remove the partial destination folders, reconnect the drive directly to a rear USB port, and recopy one top-level folder at a time. Verify each folder before moving to the next. Keep the source intact until every folder passes the test.
